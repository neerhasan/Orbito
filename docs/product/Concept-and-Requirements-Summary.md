# Initial Product Concept and Requirements Summary
Project: Orbito | Version: 0.2 | Date: 2026-11-11
Status: APPROVED (0.1 approved 2026-11-10; platform section revised 2026-11-11 at the founder's instruction)

## 1. Product concept
Orbito is a personal life operating system. It lets one person plan their life, record what they do each day, and see an honest measure of where they stand. It then explains, from their own data, why things are going well or badly. Planning and tracking are connected through one chain: Life Area, Goal, Project, Task and Focus Session.

## 2. Problem
People who want to improve their lives use separate tools for tasks, calendar, habits, sleep and notes. These tools do not connect to one another, so no tool can show how daily actions relate to long-term goals, or why progress has stalled. Planning tools generally do not analyse the user's own data. Tracking tools generally do not manage goals, projects and tasks. This statement is drawn from the founder's blueprint and from a light review of comparison articles; it has not been tested with users.

## 3. Target audience
- **Primary expected customers:** people with ADHD, as stated by the founder. Market size and willingness to pay are not verified. A published meta-analysis estimates adult ADHD prevalence at roughly 4.6% (persistent) to 8.8% (symptomatic), with very high variation between studies.
- **Positioning:** a general life app with ADHD-friendly design and features. It is not an ADHD-only app and will make no medical claims.
- **First user:** the founder, who will use the app personally before any sales begin.

## 4. Purpose and expected outcome
The user knows their true position in each area of life, knows what to do next, including on a failed day, and can see how far they have come. The approach is guided by the ideas of improving one percent every day, Atomic Habits and identity-based change. These are self-help frameworks and will not be presented in the app as scientifically proven.

## 5. Platforms
Long-term target: one application for Mac, iPhone, iPad, Windows and Android, with Apple users as the main market. First release: Windows and Android only. Apple versions (Mac, iPhone, iPad) are built later, after all features are confirmed. The web version has not been decided. The founder suggested Flutter as a single-codebase approach, which also allows Apple versions to be added later; Flutter is proposed in Technology-Stack.md. The founder owns Windows and Android devices only. Building and publishing Apple apps will need a Mac and a paid developer account, which is planned for the later Apple release.

## 6. Core capabilities (draft, to be finalised in Phase 02)
- Planning: life areas, goals, projects, tasks, calendar, notes, inbox.
- Daily tracking: habits, sleep, focus sessions, time.
- Quick Launcher: one typed line becomes a task, a log or a note.
- Life measurement: a score per life area and an overall Life line, with the real position always shown.
- Insights: patterns found in the user's own data, each labelled as Fact, Correlation, Hypothesis or Recommendation. Correlations are never presented as proven causes. Fourteen-day tests allow the user to check a hypothesis.
- Fail-day guidance: when a day goes badly, the app shows the next small step and the progress made so far.
- Energy modes: low, medium and high, each with its own routine.
- Customisable dashboard: the user chooses which sections to show in Settings.

## 7. Business model
Freemium subscription. Personal use first; sales begin only when the app is fully functional and works across platforms. Every new user receives a one-month trial of all premium features. After the trial, premium features are switched off and the free tier remains. The list of free and premium features, the price and any lifetime option are not yet decided.

## 8. Competitors and unique value proposition
Competitors named by the founder: Notion, Todoist, TickTick, Notion Calendar, Sunsama, Akiflow and Morgen. One further name ("Cal") has not been identified. Additional apps found: Tiimo, Inflow, Structured, Finch, Xenith, Discy, Evident, Exist, Gyroscope, Bearable and LifeWheel.

Working hypothesis, not yet verified: no single app found combines life areas, goals, projects, tasks, daily tracking and cause-finding insights, with an ADHD-friendly design, across Mac, iPhone, iPad, Windows and Android. Xenith and Discy are the closest apps found and need closer examination. The research relied partly on comparison articles written by competitors and was not exhaustive.

## 9. Success criteria (first year)
1. The app works for the founder every day for 90 days. The definition of "every day" is TBD.
2. First paying subscribers. The number and the date are TBD.

## 10. Risks and open questions
- Apple versions will need a Mac and a paid developer account that the founder does not currently have. This is deferred to the later Apple release (to be verified then).
- The scope is large for a solo founder. A first release must be limited in Phase 02.
- The assumption that showing the real score does not discourage ADHD users must be tested in beta.
- ADHD-related data is health-related and sensitive. Privacy requirements must be set in Phase 02 and Phase 04, and the legal rules for each target country must be verified.
- Open decisions: web version, free and premium split, price, lifetime option, calendars to sync, life-stage names and thresholds, success targets.
